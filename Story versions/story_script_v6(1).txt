---------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT CHANGED FROM v5
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Only the player's motive. The player is no longer being drawn in or made
fond of the merchant. He is a working fisherman who is cautious, curious,
and keeps fishing because it is his job.

  - Day 1 now establishes the job (the rent is due, "a fisherman who does
    not fish is only a man standing near water").
  - The player probes and files things away ("things to ask later"), and
    the merchant answers evasively.
  - As madness rises, "it is only the job" goes from a sensible reason to
    the last excuse he has left (section 11).
  - Days 2 and 3 no longer have the player drifting to the dock or walking
    without feeling his feet. He goes because he has to know.
  - The reveal's last thought changes with madness: careful, curious, or
	clinging to "it is only the job".
  - A few slips and unprompted lines lost their "proud / happy / good" wording.
  - Log edits now also rewrite Day 2 and Day 3 lines (edits 8 to 14), so the
	log keeps going wrong in what the player is reading (section 13).

---------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO READ THIS FILE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Every block starts with an EVENT ID, like d2_c8 (Day 2, catch count 8).
Use these IDs as the keys in your code.

TAGS
  [LOG]       Text the story log writes by itself. Second person, present tense.
  [THOUGHT]   The player's inner voice. Suggested: wrap in [i]...[/i] in the log.
  [MERCHANT]  Spoken dialogue. Suggested: a distinct color or leading dash.
  [SYSTEM]    Not shown as text. Something in the UI or game state changes.
              "SLIP (accident)" or "SLIP (on purpose)" marks a merchant slip.
  [TYPING]    SLOW = full typewriter treatment, FAST = quick plain line.

PLACEHOLDERS (fill these in from game state)
  {species}       species of the first abomination caught
  {heaviest}      weight of the heaviest fish caught so far
  {count}         total fish caught so far
  {pouches}       pouches the player currently holds
  {sold}          number of abominations sold so far
  [Player Name]   the player's name. If the player has none, cut those lines.

DESIGN REMINDERS
  - Pouches are flat pay: 1 per abomination, 2 per large one. Never changes.
  - Madness bar: catch +6, carry unsold +1 per cast, refuse +5, sell -8.
  - Hidden floor: every sale +2, never decreases. Bar cannot drop below it.
  - At 100 the bar triggers THE HANDS (see section 10).
  - All numbers are placeholders. Tune in playtesting.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
THE THREE RULES FOR THE MERCHANT
---------------------------------------------------------------------

1. THE LADDER. His strangeness only ever goes UP. Never remove a tell.

   LEVEL 1  (Day 1)  Odd, but the player has an answer for everything
			Arrives without a sound. Dressed too warmly. Watches the bucket,
			not you. Handles fish gently. Smiles slowly. Warm pouch.

   LEVEL 2  (Day 2)  Odd, and the answers are getting thin
			Already there before you. Dry in the rain. Footprints that stay
			wet. Knows your name. Cold hands. Never eats or drinks. A lantern
			lit by no one. Something follows the dock.

   LEVEL 3  (Day 3)  Impossible, and the player stops explaining
			A second voice under his. Water coming out of him. The lake goes
			silent for him. You never see how he takes the fish. He stops
			hiding.

   LEVEL 4  (the end)  Revealed, once. Then the game stops.

2. THE EXPLANATIONS. On Days 1 and 2, every oddity gets an ordinary
   explanation, and the player reaches for it a little too quickly. On Day 3
   the explanations run out and the player simply knows what he saw.

3. THE SLIPS. Most of the time he keeps his cool. Now and then a word, a
   pause or a habit gives him away. Early on it is by accident. Later it is
   on purpose, because he has stopped bothering. Tag every slip.

THE PLAYER'S MOTIVE (the other half of the horror)
   The player is cautious, curious, and a working man. He does not want to
   please the merchant. He keeps fishing because it is his job, because a
   buyer is a buyer, and because he has to know what the old man wants. As
   madness rises, "it is only the job" stops being a reason and becomes the
   last excuse he has left. By the end it is all he can say.

=====================================================================

Normal fish are released automatically. The merchant does not want them.

[TYPING: FAST]
- A perch, small and indignant. You return it to the water.
- A roach. It flops once and is gone.
- A trout. Good weight, good color. You let it go. You are not sure why.
- Nothing on the hook but weed. You shake it loose.
- A bream. It looks at you, offended, then sinks.
- The line comes back empty. The bait is gone.
- A pike, half asleep. It lets you unhook it without a fight.
- A small eel. You drop it quickly. It looks like a question.

[TYPING: SLOW]

---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]

4. DAY 3 - THE END OF THE DOCK (merchant level 3: no more explaining)
=====================================================================
---------------------------------------------------------------------
d3_c0   The dock under shallow water
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The dock is under an inch of water. It has not rained.
[LOG] No birds. No wind. The road behind you is empty in both directions.
[LOG] The old man stands at the very end, facing the lake. He does not turn.
[MERCHANT] "You came early. Good."
[THOUGHT] You told yourself you would only look.
[THOUGHT] You brought the rod anyway. A fisherman brings the rod.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d3_c1   Every catch is an abomination now
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The first cast is an abomination. So is the second. So is every cast, from now on.
[LOG] The lake has stopped pretending. It gives you only one kind of fish.
[THOUGHT] When did I stop being surprised?

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d3_c2   On watch
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The old man has stopped sitting. He stands at the very end of the dock now, cane planted, facing the lake, and does not turn when you cast.
[LOG] The water under the boards slaps slow and heavy, like something being patted.
[THOUGHT] He is not resting anymore. He is on watch.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d3_c3   He buys without turning. Your watch.
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] He takes the fish without turning around. You hand it over and he does not look at it, or you.
[LOG] The pouch is heavy. Inside, among the pale discs, is a wristwatch. Yours. The hands have stopped at 6:12, the minute you first saw him.
[LOG] You check your wrist. It is bare. You do not remember taking it off.
[THOUGHT] When did he take it? He has not been near me.
[THOUGHT] He has not been near me.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d3_c4   "Mine." (he does not correct it this time)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[MERCHANT] "Do you know why they come to you?"
[LOG] He means the fish. He says it without turning.
[THOUGHT] You have been wondering that for three days. It is no comfort to hear him wonder it too.
[MERCHANT] "They are not lost. They were never lost. They are only slow, and the water is very, very large."
[LOG] A long pause. The line thrums softly in the water.
[MERCHANT] "I am only collecting what is mine."
[THOUGHT] Mine. He said mine.
[THOUGHT] He did not correct it.
[SYSTEM] SLIP (on purpose): he has stopped hiding.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d3_c5   You do not see how he takes it
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] This time he turns to face you. He takes the next fish from your hands, and you do not see what he does with it. One moment it is there. The next his hand is empty and his mouth is closed, and the pouch he gives you is warm.
[MERCHANT] "Forgive the manners. It has been such a long wait."
[THOUGHT] Do not look away. If you look away, he will know you saw.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] You were never fishing.

[LOG] The line goes into the water. The line comes back out. It is only a morning.

d3_c7   The lake holds still
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The birds are gone. The insects are gone. Even the wind has stopped, as if it were waiting for permission.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

[THOUGHT] He is tired of pretending.

FOR YOUR EYES ONLY (never stated in the game)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Something vast lives in the lake, and the lake is only the part of it that
shows. The abominations are its children, lost and wandering, too afraid of
it to come home. It cannot rise until they are all returned, and they will
not come to it, so it wore the shape of an old merchant and found a
fisherman. Every cast was a lure. Every sale was a feeding. The player's
sense of duty was the lure: a working man who keeps casting because it is
his job, right up to the day the thing on the line is him.
