THE DOCK - STORY SCRIPT (v2: five days)
Editable master text for all narration, dialogue and thoughts

---------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO READ THIS FILE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Every block starts with an EVENT ID, like d3_c4 (Day 3, catch count 4).
Use these IDs as the keys in your code.

TAGS
  [LOG]       Text the story log writes by itself. Second person, present tense.
  [THOUGHT]   The player's inner voice. Suggested: wrap in [i]...[/i] in the log.
  [MERCHANT]  Spoken dialogue. Suggested: a distinct color or leading dash.
  [SYSTEM]    Not shown as text. Something in the UI or game state changes.
  [TYPING]    SLOW = full typewriter treatment, FAST = quick plain line.

PLACEHOLDERS (fill these in from game state)
  {species}       species of the first abomination caught
  {heaviest}      weight of the heaviest fish caught so far
  {count}         total fish caught so far
  {pouches}       pouches the player currently holds
  {sold}          number of abominations sold so far
  [Player Name]   the player's name. If the player has none, cut those lines.
  "Minnow"        the childhood nickname used on Day 4. Change it freely.

THE FIVE DAYS
  Day 1  THE OFFER      A normal fisherman catches a wrong fish. A stranger appears.
  Day 2  THE SHOP       The stranger sells things. The pouches get strange.
  Day 3  THE ABSENCE    He disappears. The log gets bolder. A notebook surfaces.
  Day 4  THE QUIET      He drops his guard. The world outside starts to disagree.
  Day 5  THE LAST DAY   The road is gone. Into the town, to the end.

DESIGN REMINDERS
  - Pouches are flat pay: 1 per abomination, 2 per large one. Never changes.
  - Madness bar: catch +6, carry unsold +1 per cast, refuse +5, sell -8.
  - Hidden floor: every sale +2, never decreases. Bar cannot drop below it.
  - At 100 the bar triggers THE HANDS (see section 12).
  - Ending is decided by (floor + bar) at the final cast (see section 15).
  - Day 3 is the pressure valve: the merchant is gone until d3_c8, so the
    sell button is disabled and unsold fish pile up. Let the carry tick bite.
  - Day 4 and 5: ordinary catches fade out, then stop completely.
  - After every day, play a NIGHT INTERLUDE (section 7). They are cheap,
    quiet, and give the player a breather before the next escalation.
  - All numbers are placeholders. Tune in playtesting.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
ORDINARY FISH (Days 1-3 normal, Day 4 rare, Day 5 none)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Normal fish are released automatically. The merchant does not want them.

[TYPING: FAST]
- A perch, small and indignant. You return it to the water.
- A roach. It flops once and is gone.
- A trout. Good weight, good color. You let it go. You are not sure why.
- Nothing on the hook but weed. You shake it loose.
- A bream. It looks at you, offended, then sinks.
- The line comes back empty. The bait is gone.
- A pike, half asleep. It lets you unhook it without a fight.
- A small eel. You drop it quickly. It looks like a question.
- A carp, slow and golden. It lingers at the surface as if to be thanked.
- A gudgeon. It is so ordinary it makes you smile.

ABOMINATION FILLER (FAST, use on counts that have no story beat)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

[TYPING: FAST]
- Something pale and wrong. It goes in the bucket without a sound.
- A fish with no face, only smooth skin. You do not look at it again.
- Teeth, too many, on a fish no longer than your hand. The bucket takes it.
- A thing with fins like fingers. It waves, once, and is still.
- It weighs almost nothing. It feels like carrying a held breath.
- Another. Colder than the last.
- It stares up at you from the bucket. You put the lid on.
- A long, thin shape, like an eel with a human spine. Quick and quiet.
- A fish, nearly. It hums as you lift it.
- The bucket is a little heavier. You do not count.
- It looks at the old man before it looks at you.
- It does not struggle. It has the patience of something that knows where it is going.

2. DAY 1 - THE OFFER
=====================================================================

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d1_c0   [Fishing button is shown]
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The dock creaks beneath your boots.
[LOG] Grey water stretches out to a greyer horizon. You have fished here since you were a boy.
[LOG] Your thermos is warm. Your bucket is empty. The day is yours.
[THOUGHT] Same spot, same hour. Some things you can count on.
[SYSTEM] Fishing button appears.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
d1_c3   First abomination
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The line goes heavy, then strangely light.
[LOG] What comes up is a fish, mostly. Pale as candle wax, ribs showing through its skin like the hull of a boat. Too many teeth, and they are in the wrong places.
[LOG] It is still breathing. Slowly, as if it is thinking about it.
[THOUGHT] A deformity. Pollution, maybe. You have read about this.
[LOG] You put it in the bucket. You are not sure why you did not throw it back.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
d1_c6   Second abomination. The merchant is introduced.
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] Another. Different from the first, and wrong in a different way: a single eye, wide and glassy, set in the side of its throat.
[THOUGHT] Two is not a coincidence. Two is a pattern. You don't like patterns.
[LOG] A board creaks behind you.
[LOG] You did not hear anyone walk down the dock. An old man stands a few paces away, hands folded over the head of a cane, dressed too warmly for the weather. He is looking at your bucket, not at you.
[MERCHANT] "Good water today."
[LOG] You agree that it is. He nods slowly, as if you had told him something important, and says nothing more.
[THOUGHT] Where did he come from? There is only the one road.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
d1_c7   Third abomination. The madness bar is introduced.
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The third comes up easily, almost willingly. It is the size of your hand, its scales the color of old bruises.
[LOG] Something moves behind your eyes, like a fish turning in deep water. Then it is gone.
[LOG] A thin line appears at the edge of your thoughts, and you know, without being told, what it measures.
[THOUGHT] Three. Why does it feel like I am being counted?
[SYSTEM] Madness bar is revealed.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
d1_c9   The merchant offers to buy. Sell button and pouches appear.
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The old man has been standing there a long while. When you reach for the bucket, he finally speaks.
[MERCHANT] "Selling today?"
[LOG] You tell him they are not for sale. They are not even fit to eat.
[MERCHANT] "No. They are not."
[LOG] He smiles. It takes some time.
[MERCHANT] "I will take all of them. Whatever you have caught, whatever you catch. I pay well, and I pay in what is useful."
[LOG] He holds out a small pouch of cracked leather. It is heavier than it looks.
[THOUGHT] Someone wants these. Someone will pay. Why?
[THOUGHT] It is only money. Does it matter?
[SYSTEM] Sell button appears. Pouches appear in the UI.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
d1_c10   The merchant's greed begins to show
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The old man has moved closer without you noticing. His eyes follow every cast.
[LOG] When your line breaks the surface he leans, just slightly, like a plant toward light.
[MERCHANT] "Another one soon. I can feel it. Can't you?"
[THOUGHT] I can't. But I almost say I can.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
d1_c11   Evening begins. Bigger abominations available.
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The light goes orange, then red, then something without a name. The water darkens early.
[LOG] The old man's shadow on the planks is longer than he is. It reaches the end of the dock and keeps going.
[LOG] Beneath you, something large turns over in its sleep. The boards hum with it.
[THOUGHT] Time to go home.
[LOG] You do not go home.
[SYSTEM] Large abominations now possible.

d1_c13   Small abomination. The merchant snatches it and eats it raw.
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] It is small. Barely a mouthful. It has no eyes at all, only smooth skin where they should be, and it is screaming without sound.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The rod bends double. It takes everything you have, and when it clears the water you almost let go.
[LOG] It is longer than your arm. Its skin is slick and mottled like a drowned thing, and when the mouth opens there is no end to it.
[LOG] A shadow falls across your shoulder. You did not hear him come.
[LOG] You can smell him: lake water and old stone and something sweet, like fruit left too long.
[MERCHANT] (low, close to your ear) "Beautiful."
[THOUGHT] Do not turn around.
[LOG] You set the fish down on the planks. You do not look at him. You walk, without running, to the end of the dock and up the road, and you do not stop until your door is shut and locked.

3. DAY 2 - THE SHOP
=====================================================================

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d2_c0   Fog. The fish is gone. The merchant's shop opens.
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] Fog. You can barely see the water, but you can hear it, lapping close.
[LOG] The fish you left on the dock is gone. There are drag marks across the wet planks, leading over the edge. None lead back.
[LOG] The old man is already seated at the end of the dock. His coat is dry. Everything else is damp.
[LOG] He has spread a cloth beside him and laid out a few things on it: a coil of line, a lantern, a bucket with a lid.
[MERCHANT] "Good morning. I took the liberty of keeping your catch safe."
[THOUGHT] Safe from what?
[SYSTEM] Shop opens.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
d2_c1   A normal catch, and relief
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] A perch. Plain and ordinary. You nearly laugh with relief.
[LOG] The old man does not look at it.
[MERCHANT] "Not those."
[THOUGHT] He says it like a man declining a gift he has been given many times.


---------------------------------------------------------------------

---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The water gives it up as though it was waiting by the hook.
[LOG] Grey, pebbled skin. Fins like torn sails. It looks at you with something very close to recognition.
[THOUGHT] It knows me. That is ridiculous.
IF unsold >= 2:
[LOG] The bucket begins to hum. A low sound, almost a tune. The fish inside shift when you look away.
[THOUGHT] Count them again. Count them again.
[SYSTEM] Log skips one line (a missing line is intentional).


---------------------------------------------------------------------
d2_c4   Heavier line. First purchase.
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The old man taps the coil of line with his cane.
[MERCHANT] "The deeper ones will not rise to a thin line. This will hold."
[LOG] He names a price: two pouches. It does not sound like a price. It sounds like a courtesy.
[THOUGHT] The deeper ones. He says it like he knows them.
[MERCHANT] "They are the lost ones, you understand. They only want to come home."
[SYSTEM] Heavy Line available (2 pouches).


---------------------------------------------------------------------
d2_c6   Abomination with a human mouth
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The mouth is wrong the moment you see it. Soft, lipped, almost human. It opens and closes, opens and closes.
[LOG] It takes you a long moment to understand that it is breathing in time with you.
[THOUGHT] Hold your breath. See what it does.
[LOG] You do not hold your breath.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
d2_c7   He greets you by name
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] He watches you cast. Then, mildly, as though continuing a long conversation:
[MERCHANT] "You are tired, [Player Name]."
[LOG] You do not recall telling him your name.
[THOUGHT] The pouch. The sign above the bait shop. Someone at the bar. There are a dozen ways he could know.
[THOUGHT] You do not believe any of them.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
d2_c8   Rain that avoids him
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] Rain begins, thin and cold. It falls on the dock, the water, your hood, your hands.
[LOG] It does not fall on him. A dry circle follows him like a held breath.
[LOG] You look down. His footprints on the wet boards are dark, and do not fade.
[THOUGHT] Do not stare. He will notice you staring.
[MERCHANT] "It is only weather."


---------------------------------------------------------------------
d2_c9   Lore 1: the first catch
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The old man speaks without being asked.
[MERCHANT] "Your town used to leave the first catch of every season on this dock. A courtesy. The best fish, left out for the water."
[MERCHANT] "Nobody remembers why. They only know it was bad luck to forget."
[THOUGHT] Grandad used to do that. I never asked him why either.
[MERCHANT] "The water remembers. The water is patient. It has had a great deal of time."


---------------------------------------------------------------------
d2_c10   Small abomination. The coin in the pouch.
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] A small thing, quick and slippery, with a jaw that unfolds like a fan.
[LOG] You sell it. The pouch is warm. You open it, because you finally have to.
[LOG] The coin inside is not a coin. It is a scale, pale and curved. It pulses once against your palm, like a slow heartbeat.
[THOUGHT] Close the pouch. Close it. It is only money.
[LOG] You close the pouch.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
d2_c11   Evening. The tide goes out too far.
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The tide drops, and keeps dropping, farther than a tide has any right to. The posts of the dock stand exposed, crusted with shapes you do not look at for long.
[LOG] The water that remains is flat and black and very still.
[THOUGHT] It is waiting.
[SYSTEM] Large abominations now possible.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
d2_c12   Conditional: only if the player has refused at least once
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[MERCHANT] "I can wait."
[LOG] He says it quietly, and does not say anything else. His smile has stayed on his face a moment too long.
(If the player has NOT refused yet, skip this beat or use a FAST filler.)


---------------------------------------------------------------------
d2_c13   Large abomination, still alive. His hands tremble.
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] It is enormous and it is still alive. It thrashes on the planks, slow and heavy, and one eye rolls toward you.
[LOG] The eye is the same pale color as the old man's.
[THOUGHT] No. Not the same. Just a trick of the fog.
[LOG] He buys it without haggling. When he lifts it, his hands tremble. Just once, just slightly, a thing a more careful man would have hidden.
[MERCHANT] "Excuse me. I am not what I was."


---------------------------------------------------------------------
d2_c14   "Tomorrow I may not be here." End of Day 2.
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The old man stands. His knees do not crack, the way an old man's should.
[MERCHANT] "Tomorrow I may not be here. Do not be alarmed."
[LOG] He says it as though it were a small, practical matter, like the closing of a shop.
[MERCHANT] "I am always nearer than you think."
[LOG] You walk home. In the window of your kitchen, your reflection is wet, though you never touched the water.
[THOUGHT] Do not look at it. Do not look at it for long.
[SYSTEM] Day 2 ends. Night interlude 2, then Day 3.

4. DAY 3 - THE ABSENCE
=====================================================================
---------------------------------------------------------------------
d3_c0   The empty dock. Shop open, merchant absent.
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The fog is gone. The sky is clear and hard and blue, and the lake lies flat as a plate.
[LOG] The end of the dock is empty. The cloth is still there, the line and the lantern and the bucket with its lid laid out in the same neat row, untouched by the dew.
[LOG] There are no footprints in the damp. There is no sign anyone sat here at all.
[THOUGHT] Good. That is good.
[THOUGHT] Why is it quiet like this?
[SYSTEM] Sell button disabled until d3_c8. Unsold fish accumulate and the carry tick keeps running.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d3_c1   Relief, and something worse than relief
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] A trout. Plain, bright, muscular, a fish that wants to live. You hold it a moment longer than you need to.
[LOG] You put it back. It does not swim away at once. It hangs just below the surface, watching.
[THOUGHT] You almost miss him.
[THOUGHT] Ridiculous. You do not miss him.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d3_c2   (filler)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d3_c3   Abomination. No one to take it.
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] It comes up the moment the line is in the water, as if it had been waiting beside the hook.
[LOG] Hairless, flat, hands where the fins should be. They open and close on the planks, feeling for something.
[LOG] When you set it down it drags itself, hand over hand, to the empty end of the dock and stops there, facing the cloth.
[THOUGHT] It is looking for him.
[THOUGHT] It knows where he sits.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d3_c4   The notebook
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The line catches on something that is not a fish. A weight, flat and square, wrapped in weed.
[LOG] It is a notebook, bound in leather gone black and soft with water. The pages should have pulped. They have not.
[LOG] The first page is in handwriting you know. It takes a moment to place. It is yours.
[LOG] "The dock creaks beneath your boots."
[THOUGHT] I have never written this. I have never seen this book.
[THOUGHT] Close it.
[LOG] You do not close it.
[SYSTEM] Notebook item gained. See section 13 for what its pages say at each madness tier.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d3_c5   His seat
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] You walk to the end of the dock, to the place where he sits. The boards there are dry and warm, shaped to a body that is not there.
[LOG] Your own footprints cross the dew behind you. You look for his. There are none, not coming, not going.
[THOUGHT] He did not walk away.
[THOUGHT] He was never walking.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d3_c6   The bell
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] A bell rings, somewhere under the water.
[LOG] One long note, slow and deep, and then a silence so complete you hear your own pulse. The surface of the lake shivers in rings, as though the sound had weight.
[THOUGHT] There is no church on this shore.
[THOUGHT] There was one once. Grandad used to say so.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d3_c7   Large abomination. The brass button.
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The rod bends, and keeps bending. It is a long, slow haul, and when it comes up it is enormous, wide and white as a drowned sail.
[LOG] Something glints in its jaw. You pry it out. A brass button, green with age, the kind from a heavy coat.
[THOUGHT] Whose coat.
[THOUGHT] Do not put it in your pocket.
[LOG] You put it in your pocket.
[SYSTEM] Flag: has_button = true (pays off in ending B).

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d3_c8   He returns
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[MERCHANT] "You have been reading."
[LOG] He is seated at the end of the dock, coat dry, hands folded on his cane. The cloth is exactly where it was. You did not hear him arrive. You did not look away.
[MERCHANT] "Every fisherman writes, in the end. The lake likes to be written. It is the only way it knows anyone has been."
[THOUGHT] He says it so gently.
[MERCHANT] "Keep it, if you like. It ends the same way for all of us."
[SYSTEM] Merchant returns. Sell button re-enabled.

d3_c9   A child's tooth (fire on the first sale of the day)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] He pays for the first one with more care than usual. The pouch is warm, and inside it, among the scales, is a tooth.
[LOG] Small. White. A child's tooth, milk-pale, with a thread of root still on it.
[THOUGHT] Do not think about whose.
[MERCHANT] "They are only keepsakes. Everyone who passes through leaves a little. It would be unkind to throw them out."

[TYPING: SLOW]

[THOUGHT] Do not look down. Do not look down.

[LOG] You raise your hand. The figure across the water raises his, at the same moment, at the same height.

=====================================================================
5. DAY 4 - THE QUIET
=====================================================================
---------------------------------------------------------------------
d4_c0   The empty bucket. His humming.
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The bucket is empty.
[LOG] The lid is off. The bottom is wet, and a long clean line of damp leads across your kitchen floor, out of the door and down the road, toward the water.
[LOG] You follow it, because you cannot think what else to do. It ends at the dock.
[LOG] He is at the end, back turned, humming. It is a slow tune with no words, one your grandmother sang when you were small. You have not thought of it in thirty years.
[THOUGHT] The bucket was humming it too. That is where I know it from.
[THOUGHT] No. That is not where.
[SYSTEM] Carried fish removed, no sale. The carry tick resets.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d4_c1   The last ordinary catch
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] A perch. The last of them, though you do not know it yet.
[LOG] It is thin and tired and it does not fight. When you let it go it sinks straight down without turning, like a coin.
[THOUGHT] There were more of them yesterday.
[SYSTEM] From here, ordinary catches are rare (about 1 in 6). Day 5: none.

d4_c2   (filler)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d4_c3   They are glad
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The hook sets almost before it hits the water. The line does not run away from the dock. It runs toward the end of it, toward him.
[LOG] You haul back with both hands. It hauls back harder, not to escape, but to arrive.
[LOG] When it lands on the planks it flops to his feet and lies there, shivering, like a dog.
[THOUGHT] They are not afraid of him.
[THOUGHT] They are glad.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d4_c4   "What are you?"
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The words are out before you can stop them.
[THOUGHT] What are you?
[LOG] You did not say it aloud. You are almost sure you did not say it aloud.
[MERCHANT] "Patient."
[LOG] He says it as one would give a name.
[MERCHANT] "That is all, really. Everything else is only the shape I have had to wear while I waited."

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d4_c5   The handshake
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] He holds out his hand, to seal the day's bargain, as men do.
[LOG] His palm is cold and wet and perfectly smooth, like a stone from the bottom of a river. It does not feel like skin. It feels like being held.
[LOG] When you let go, your hand stays damp, and wrinkled, and pale, as if you had held it under water for hours.
[THOUGHT] Do not wipe it on your coat. He will see.
[LOG] You wipe it on your coat.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d4_c6   The reflection
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] You lean out over the water to look at the weed on the posts.
[LOG] Your reflection is there, and beside it stands another, taller, older, bending close to your shoulder.
[LOG] You look up. He is still seated at the end of the dock, twenty feet away, not moving.
[LOG] You look down. He is beside you in the water, and he is smiling.
[THOUGHT] He is not there. He is not there.
[THOUGHT] He is not only there.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
d4_c7   Large abomination. "Hush."
---------------------------------------------------------------------
[TYPING: SLOW]
[LOG] The great pale shape surfaces and rolls onto the boards, enormous and exhausted.
[LOG] The old man rises, which he has not done in all the days you have known him. He kneels beside it. He lays his palm on its brow.
[MERCHANT] (very softly) "Hush. Hush now. It is over. You have come such a long way."
[LOG] The fish goes still. Its eye closes, slowly, with something like gratitude.
[THOUGHT] He loved it. He actually loved it.
[THOUGHT] That is worse than if he had not.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

EDITING NOTES
---------------------------------------------------------------------

- Keep sentences short for the early game, so madness distortions are
  noticeable. If the opening prose is already ornate, glitches will not stand out.
- Use the merchant's lines sparingly. He is most unsettling when he says
  less than you expect.
- Never explain what the merchant is. Every lore beat should raise a
  question, not answer one.
- The log is the lake's voice. That is why it sounds like him the more you
  sell. Do not state this in the game.
- Reuse the opening line "The dock creaks beneath your boots." at the very
  end of the High ending. Do not alter it elsewhere except in EDIT 1.
- Intentional contradiction: the woman on Day 4 says nobody has used the
  dock in forty years, while the player has fished there since a boy. Leave
  it unexplained.
- The hook, the key and the childhood name are the personal escalations.
  They matter because they make the lake feel like it has been waiting
  for this specific player. Do not add more than these three.
- The far dock (d3_c12) is the player seen from outside. Never confirm it.
