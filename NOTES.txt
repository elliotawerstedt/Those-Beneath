Should selling lower madness?

Yes, but only partly. If selling reset the bar, the log would rarely get to show its personality, and the ending wouldn’t depend on what you did. So use two values:

The bar (visible): goes up from catching abominations, carrying unsold ones, and refusing him. Selling lowers it, and that relief is why the player wants to sell.
The floor (hidden): every sale raises it a little, and it never goes down. The bar can’t drop below it. Selling feels like relief, but each sale takes a bit more of you, so the player is trapped by the thing that helps.

Placeholder numbers to tune: catch +6, carried unsold +1 per cast, refuse +5, sale -8 on the bar and +2 on the floor. At 100% The Hands take over: they hand everything over for 0 pouches, the bar resets to 60, and the floor gains +5. The ending is based on floor + bar at the final cast.

Pouches stay flat (1 per abomination, 2 per large), so there’s no price to farm.

The log’s personality

The log isn’t a narrator. It’s the story writing itself, so it misbehaves in ways that read as the game itself coming apart. Tiers key off the bar:

0-24, Reliable: Plain, short, accurate.
25-49, Hesitant: It stutters mid-word, repeats a sentence, and quietly changes one word in an old line.
50-74, Self-correcting: It strikes words out (BBCode [s]) and rewrites them, slips tense and pronouns, and contradicts earlier entries. It calls the merchant different things: “the old man”, then “the host”, then “him”.
75-99, Taking initiative: It edits past lines while you watch, writes unprompted lines, lets the merchant’s speech leak into your actions, drops punctuation, and says “we” instead of “you”.
100, The Hands: It writes the handover as your own decision and erases one of your earlier lines.

More ways the log develops a personality:

Callbacks: It quotes your own catches back at you, like the heaviest fish, how many you’ve caught, and the species of the first abomination.
Mood from your choices: After a sale it turns warm and slow, almost satisfied. After a refusal it gets cold and clipped, and it sulks.
Hunger: The more you sell, the more its phrasing resembles the merchant’s. By the end it sounds like him.
Lies: At 50+, it sometimes reports a wrong weight or count.
Silence: When he speaks at high madness, the log writes nothing and leaves a blank gap.

Refusal escalation: First, “I can wait.” Second, the water laps higher and his voice has two tones. Third, the dock is wet to your ankles. He never threatens.

Hidden lore: The lake was a sea. Something old sank beneath the dock, and the abominations are pieces the water keeps trying to return. The merchant is the part of it that walked out to collect them, and every fisherman before you collected too. The log is the lake’s voice. That’s why it sounds more like him the more you sell.

Endings
Low, The Release: He thanks you, walks into the lake, and the water closes. You never catch another fish, but every morning a scale sits on your pillow.
Mid, The Succession: He hands you his coat, and you realize he was a fisherman once. A new fisherman steps onto the dock and you hear yourself say, “Selling today?”
High, The Reveal: He drops the disguise and the log stops following events. It writes faster and faster in words that aren’t yours, then loops back to your first line: “The dock creaks beneath your boots.”
